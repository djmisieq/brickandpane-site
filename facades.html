Facades & roof glazing

Curtain walling, roof lanterns, rooflights and shopfronts.

Curtain walling for glazed gables, stair towers and double-height rooms. Roof lanterns and rooflights that bring daylight into deep London plans. Shopfronts for mixed-use schemes. Most of the risk sits in the junctions with the structure, the roof and the walls, so we draw those with you before anything is made.

Curtain walling

Mullions from c. 50 mm · capped or structurally glazed · opening vents and doors

A grid of mullions and transoms carrying large panes, hung off the structure rather than sitting in a wall opening. We use stick systems, built on site, which suit houses and small schemes: glazed gables, stair towers, double-height living rooms and glazed links between old and new.

  • Capped, ridge-capped or silicone-jointed faces
  • Integrated doors, top-hung and parallel-opening vents
  • Timber-aluminium option with timber mullions inside
  • Aluminium
  • Timber

Roof lanterns

Up to c. 3 × 6 m · slim aluminium rafters · solar control glass

The classic answer to a dark middle room in a terrace extension. A pitched glass lantern on a flat roof brings in far more daylight than a wall window of the same area. We size the glass for solar control so the kitchen doesn’t overheat in summer.

  • Self-cleaning and solar control coatings
  • Insulated upstand, usually at least 150 mm above the roof finish
  • Timber lanterns for traditional orangeries
  • Aluminium

Flat rooflights

Fixed · opening · roof access hatches

Flat glass over a kitchen or an extension, an electrically opening rooflight for night cooling, or a sliding hatch onto a roof terrace. A laminated inner pane keeps broken glass in place.

  • Rain sensors on opening rooflights
  • Hidden frames from inside
  • Aluminium

Shopfronts and entrances

Ground-floor commercial units · communal entrances

Glazed shopfronts and communal entrance screens for mixed-use and residential schemes. For developers we can supply them in the same package as the flat windows and doors above, with one programme.

What we check before we draw

Facades and roof glazing touch more of the building than any window. These are the points we settle at Stage 4, not on site.

Part L

Energy

New rooflights in existing homes need a U-value of 2.2 W/m²K or better (see roof lantern or rooflight?). In extensions, large glazed areas count towards the 25% allowance, or need a whole-dwelling calculation.

Part O

Overheating

New homes must show they won’t overheat. Solar control glass, g-values and opening rooflights all feed into that assessment, and we provide the figures.

Part K

Safety

Safety glass in critical locations, guarding where glazing protects a drop, and a laminated inner pane in roof glazing.

Structure

Loads and movement

Curtain walling is fixed back to the frame. We agree brackets, deflection limits and movement joints with your engineer before production.

Testing

Tested systems only

Curtain walling to EN 13830, with air, water and wind-load classes from the system’s test reports. Installation follows CWCT guidance.

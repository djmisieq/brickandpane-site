Windows

Casement, sash, tilt & turn and steel-look windows, made for the house.

A Victorian terrace, a 1930s semi and a new-build mews need different windows. We make casement, tilt & turn, sliding sash, steel-look and fixed windows in aluminium, timber and PVC, and help you pick what the building, the planners and the budget will accept.

Casement windows

Side-hung or top-hung · flush or stepped sash · top lights

The everyday British window. Sashes open outwards on friction stays, so they don’t take up room inside. A flush sash sits level with the frame for a cleaner, more traditional face. Stepped sashes are simpler and cost less.

  • Easy-clean hinges for upper floors
  • Escape hinges for first-floor bedrooms
  • Trickle vents fitted on the frame
  • Aluminium
  • Timber
  • PVC

Tilt & turn windows

Opens inwards · tilt for air, turn to clean · large sashes

One handle, two ways to open. Tilt the top in for secure ventilation, or turn the whole sash in to clean the outside from the room. Ideal for flats and upper floors, and for big single panes where outward-opening sashes would be too heavy.

  • Cleaning without ladders or access equipment
  • Night ventilation that stays secure
  • Restrictors for Part O and child safety
  • Timber
  • PVC

Sliding sash windows

Slides up and down to open · horns · glazing bars

London’s signature window: two sashes that slide up and down in the frame. Timber for period houses and conservation areas, PVC where the planners allow it.

  • Like-for-like replacement or new sashes in old boxes
  • Draught-sealed sashes
  • Slimline glazing for listed and conservation buildings

Steel-look windows

Slim sightlines · bold glazing bars · matching doors

The industrial look of old steel windows, in thermally broken aluminium. It suits warehouse conversions and modern extensions, and can replace original metal windows where the planners will accept aluminium, without the cold frames and condensation of single-glazed steel. See replacing steel windows in a conservation area.

  • Matching doors and fixed lights
  • Any RAL colour, usually black or bronze
  • Aluminium

Loft-style glazed screens

Internal partitions · slim black frames · fixed or with a door

Glazed walls inside the house, with the slim frames and glazing bars of a loft. They divide a kitchen from a living room or close off a study without blocking the light.

  • Fixed screens or screens with a matching door
  • Any RAL colour, usually black
  • Aluminium
  • Timber
  • PVC

Fixed and picture windows

Window seats · shaped heads

Where you want a view, not a vent. Fixed panes can be much larger than opening sashes and have the slimmest frames. We combine them with opening sashes or vents so the room still meets the ventilation rules.

What Building Regulations apply to new windows in London?

We check these at drawing review and say in writing how each window complies. Planning comes first: in a conservation area or a listed building, you may need consent before anything else.

Planning

Conservation areas and listed buildings

Listed buildings need listed building consent for new windows. Many London conservation areas have Article 4 directions that remove the right to change windows on front elevations without permission.

Part L

Energy

Replacement windows in existing homes need a U-value of 1.4 W/m²K or better, or Window Energy Rating band B. New homes are designed to a notional 1.2 W/m²K. More in replacing windows: the rules in plain English.

Part B

Escape windows

First-floor habitable rooms usually need an escape window: a clear opening of at least 0.33 m², at least 450 mm wide and high, with the bottom no more than 1,100 mm above the floor.

Part F

Ventilation

Replacing windows must not reduce the background ventilation a room had before. We fit trickle vents on the frame where they are needed, sized for the room.

Part K & O

Safety and overheating

Safety glass in low and critical locations, guarding for low sills, and restrictors where Part O limits openings in new homes.

Part Q

Security in new homes

Ground-floor and easily reached windows in new dwellings must meet the security requirements of Approved Document Q. Tell us if your project is a new dwelling.

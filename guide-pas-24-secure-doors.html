PAS 24 and Part Q for entrance, sliding and bi-fold doors

“PAS 24” appears on most door quotations, but it means different things on different paperwork. Here is when the security rules apply, which doors and windows they cover, and what evidence a building control officer, or you, should ask for.

The short answer

  • Part Q applies to new dwellings, including flats and houses created by converting another building. It doesn’t apply to replacement doors or to extensions of an existing home.
  • It covers easily accessible doors and windows: roughly, anything within 2 m of the ground, a basement, an access balcony or a low flat roof.
  • The usual route is PAS 24: a door or window made to a design that has been tested to it. The test has to match what you are buying: the same system, configuration and hardware.

1. When Part Q applies

Approved Document Q supports requirement Q1 of the Building Regulations: reasonable provision must be made to resist unauthorised access to a dwelling, and to any part of a building from which a flat can be reached. Q1 applies only to new dwellings. That includes dwellings formed by a material change of use, such as offices or a shop converted to flats.

It doesn’t apply when you replace a door in an existing home, or add an extension. PAS 24 is still worth asking for in those cases.

2. Which doors and windows

Part Q covers doors and windows that are easily accessible and lead into a dwelling from outside, into a building with flats, or into a flat from the common parts. Approved Document Q defines easily accessible as either:

  • a window or doorway, any part of which is within 2 m vertically of an accessible level surface such as the ground, a basement level or an access balcony, or
  • a window within 2 m vertically of a flat or sloping roof (pitch under 30°) that is itself within 3.5 m of the ground.

That catches almost every ground-floor door and window, including sliding and bi-fold doors to a garden, easily accessible rooflights, and garage doors where the garage connects to the house. If a garage has an interconnecting door into the house, either the garage door or the interconnecting door must be secure.

The main entrance door also needs a door viewer, unless there is clear glass in or beside the door, and a door chain or limiter. Letter plates should have an opening no larger than 260 × 40 mm and be designed so keys can’t be fished out.

3. What PAS 24 is

PAS 24 is the British Standards Institution’s specification for enhanced security of doorsets and windows. It sets out attack tests, such as manipulation of locks, forcing of the leaf and removal of the glass, that a sample of the product must pass in a test laboratory. The current edition is PAS 24:2022. Approved Document Q still names the 2012 edition and accepts other standards with similar or better performance, such as STS 201 and LPS 1175 for doorsets.

Two details matter in practice:

  • A doorset is tested, not a lock. PAS 24 applies to the complete assembly: frame, leaves, glass, hardware and any integral side panel or fanlight. A PAS 24 lock in an untested door is not a PAS 24 door.
  • Fixing matters too. Approved Document Q asks for frames to be fixed mechanically to the structure, following the manufacturer’s instructions. In lightweight framed walls, a resilient layer such as 9 mm timber sheathing runs the full door height and 600 mm either side.

4. Sliding and bi-fold doors

PAS 24:2022 covers sliding doorsets (single and multi-track) and folding-sliding doorsets as well as hinged doors. Garden doors need the same evidence as the front door. For sliders and bi-folds, check:

  • The configuration. A test on a two-panel slider doesn’t automatically cover a four-panel one, a corner opening or a pocket door. Approved Document Q warns that small differences in construction can significantly affect the result.
  • Size and glass. The evidence should cover the panel sizes and the glass build-up you’re using.
  • Hardware. The locking points, handle and cylinder in the test are the ones you should get.

For the other things a big slider has to meet, see the design guide to large sliding doors.

5. What evidence to ask for

Evidence for a PAS 24 door or window, strongest first.
EvidenceWhat it shows
Third-party certificationA UKAS-accredited body certifies the product and audits production. Approved Document Q accepts such schemes.
Test reportA UKAS-accredited lab tested this design. Check that the report covers your configuration, sizes and hardware.
“Designed to PAS 24”Not evidence on its own. Ask for the report behind it.

Approved Document Q adds a warning worth repeating: evidence passed from one company to another can become unreliable if important details are lost. Ask for the document that names the product you are buying.

Questions

Do replacement doors have to meet PAS 24?

Not under Part Q, which applies only to new dwellings, including conversions. It is still worth asking for on replacement doors.

Does Part Q apply to an extension?

No. Approved Document Q applies to new dwellings only. An extension to an existing house is not a new dwelling.

Can sliding and bi-fold doors be PAS 24?

Yes. PAS 24:2022 covers sliding and folding-sliding doorsets. The test evidence has to match the configuration, sizes, glass and hardware you are buying.

What does easily accessible mean in Part Q?

A door or window any part of which is within 2 m vertically of the ground, a basement level or an access balcony, or a window within 2 m of a flat or low-pitched roof that is within 3.5 m of the ground.
